multimodal wayfinding for smart pitstops

overview

On The Way is a GPS system that provides optimal navigation from point A to point B with the ability to add up to three stops. Just plug in your destination, choose a route, and head off on your journey. Search for a stop or choose from six categories for suggestions near your location. Clear decision-making guidance won't cause you frustration while you're behind the wheel. With On The Way, it's easy to discover what lies ahead.

I noticed the problem on a trip to the Outer Banks with my family. We were using one phone's GPS for directions to our hotel, and another phone for a stop we wanted to make. As we searched for stops, we kept getting suggestions for places behind us, which would force us to drive in the opposite direction of our hotel. I wanted a platform that only recommended stops in the direction I was heading while staying close to the main route. When I started working in my current role, my supervisor assigned me a project to gauge my knowledge of UX/UI. I needed to learn Angular and build an application that somehow involved maps. I'd been sitting on this idea for years, and I felt it was the perfect time to bring it to fruition.

final design

On The Way: Discover What Lies Ahead. This application is not fully developed, so this is not a full implementation.

user research

Conducting a competitor review was integral to learning where popular GPS applications were lacking and how On The Way could be built better. Upon reviewing Apple Maps, Google Maps, and Waze, I found a number of issues. Apple Maps and Waze only allow for one stop to be added to the main route. There is no way to search for an address when adding a stop in Apple Maps; instead, users are restricted to categories like cafés, gas stations, and banks. In all three apps, pressing 'end navigation' ends navigation to all routes rather than ending the added stop and continuing the main route. The competitor review revealed flaws in existing GPS system design. I aimed to fix these flaws and create a more satisfactory user experience with On The Way.

Generating contextual use scenarios that described how someone might use On The Way helped me define potential users. Who needed directions from point A to point B? Students going to college? Families going on road trips? Businesspeople driving to important meetings? I built three distinct personas to represent each of these groups. The product needed to generate directions from point A to point B, add multiple stops along the route, search for stop addresses, keep stops close to the main route, and end navigation clearly.

ideation and conceptual design

I created a prototype with the Erin Wyman persona in mind. It depicts Erin getting directions to her house in Blacksburg from an address (as opposed to her current location), saving the Blacksburg address to her favorite places, adding a stop at a Shaky's less than 25 miles from the route, and adding another stop at a gas station. On The Way's final design varies slightly from the prototype due to some limitations with the Google Maps API.

Creating a logo and defining a style guide were some of my favorite aspects of the design process. At first, I wanted to incorporate a shark into the logo since sharks are the only fish species that cannot swim backwards. This fun fact goes with the app's theme of discovering what lies ahead; however, I found it difficult to incorporate sharks into the final design of the product so I dropped the idea and created a more straightforward design.

reflection

I experienced a lot of firsts building On The Way: my first Angular application, my first time working with the Google Maps API, my first style guide. I enjoyed every part of the design process, from ideation to implementation. Part of what makes On The Way so meaningful is that I built it from scratch. Seeing my vision brought to life has been incredible. I fully intend to revisit this project when I have more time.
