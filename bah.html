my latest work

designing a mission-critical operating tool

overview

In my latest role as a UX designer and product owner at Booz Allen Hamilton, I led design on a 25-person interdisciplinary team to deliver a high-stakes application for the United States European Command. As a product owner, I used my subject matter expertise about the mission set and user base to provide operational context to the development team and make decisions about app direction. As a designer, I implemented what users actually needed to do their jobs, not what other people asked for on their behalf. I traveled internationally to see how users really interact with the application and to note pain points that video calls and emails can't capture. I compared mockups to development builds, flagged styling issues in pull requests, and used my technical knowledge to provide solutions, cutting down the time it took for developers to address design feedback. My unique skill set closed the gap between product decisions, my designs, and the final deliverable.

The application I worked on supports planning, operations, and crisis management for users with different workflows and definitions of success. When we moved to a cloud platform, I used that as an opportunity to redesign the product around user feedback. I can't share details of this project, but I can walk through the parts of my role that best represent how I work.

Research and enablement

I talked to key stakeholders and real users to keep product design aligned with how the application was expected to provide mission support. I worked with senior leaders in small-group feedback sessions to gather input on direction and priorities. I led familiarization sessions with users to build an understanding of how the app should be used to meet mission requirements, and I hosted hands-on trainings so people could work through real tasks rather than sitting through a demo. I also ran one-on-one usability tests with brand new users to gauge whether the interface was intuitive enough to use without a specialist in the room. I created enablement assets like user guides and training videos so what was practiced in these sessions would sustain beyond the room.

Design process

Our team was a rapid-prototyping shop, so I did not spend much time in low-fidelity wireframes. I went straight to high-fidelity mockups and iterated on them continuously with the team. I created hundreds of screens that mapped every user scenario and every interactive state, specifying the experience click by click instead of leaving a happy path for someone else to fill in. Those mockups were intentional and treated as the source of truth: layout, hierarchy, and interaction were fully specified, which left little room for interpretation in development. I also built a design system for the application, including a style guide with color palettes and typography scales, and reusable components for buttons, toggles, form fields, modals, and in-app navigation so the interface stayed consistent as new scenarios were added.

Design problems I faced

planning

I redesigned the planning interface to address friction points around data entry. Users had to build a table one row at a time, then open a modal to edit and save each line. I replaced those steps with import/export and auto-population using what the system already knew about the mission set so planners could finish the task faster.

To simplify the way users input information from their source material, I mirrored those source documents in the interface so the layout matched what users were used to, leaving no room for error.

I also broke the planning section into distinct pages with clear status indicators so the shared workspace always reflected the most up-to-date information and no required field went untouched.

operations

I highlighted at-a-glance operational progress to show what already happened, what's happening now, and what to expect next.

An ineffective donut chart was the main visualization for task progression, so I redesigned how on-ground actions were tracked, making status easy to scan and understand.

To keep updates from getting buried, I added a toast notification system, which evolved into a static activity feed, to surface important changes and keep the operational picture up-to-date.

crisis management

In a crisis, senior leaders need to make quick decisions, so the goal was to create a single strategic dashboard to absorb information at a glance. Different users wanted different things from the same tool, so I had to decide what earned a place on the strategic view and what would only slow people down.
