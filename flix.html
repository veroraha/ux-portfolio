curated playlists to encourage media discovery

overview

Flixtape is an in-app Netflix feature for compiling, personalizing, and sharing playlists of favorite films and shows. Netflix launched its own version in 2016, but weak search, limited cover art, and too few titles left people unsatisfied—and the service was shut down. As a binge-watcher who wanted a personal collection, I set out to revive Flixtape in a way that still feels like Netflix.

user research

Because almost every official trace of the original product had disappeared, I built the brief from surviving reviews and articles. Quotes went into an affinity diagram—About Flixtape, User Needs, and User Issues—so I could see what to drop, what to change, and what I could not control. The requirements were clear: unlimited titles per playlist, Netflix-made lists you can add, mixed-series episode playlists, shuffle, and custom names and covers.

reflection

Ideation asked how playlists should sit in the Netflix UI: a cover versus a row of titles, what the playlist menu should do, and how adding a movie should differ from adding a single episode. The first iteration, a “My Netflix” tab, still missed the mark—no cover art, a wordy menu, tiny checkboxes, and a confusing share-permissions model. The redesigned system gives people more autonomy than the original, which suggested names, content, and covers for them. Looking ahead, Flixtape could grow into a social layer—what friends are watching, how they rate titles, playlists made together—a service no major streamer currently offers.
